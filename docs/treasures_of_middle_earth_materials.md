## 6.1 Gems

Men and Dwarves use gemstones primarily as adornment for items of power rather than as the sole focus of the Essence. Only the Jewel-smiths of the Eldar forged with crystalline materials regularly.

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| Adamant | Made only by Celebrimbor among the High Jewel Smiths. Harder than diamond or laen, yet colorable like laen and faceted like diamond — clear, brilliant, brighter than diamond. Used as the setting-stone of Nenya, Galadriel's Ring. |
| Diamond | Most prized by Men and Dwarves; the Mírdain find white stones "dull." Elves covet the very scarce colored varieties, especially blues and pale violets. |
| Emerald | Called "Elfstones" by mortals; favored by the Firstborn for their bright green color, often set in jewelry. |
| Laen ("Long Thread") | Crystal lattice structure gives great strength. Gains rigidity with heat; must be chilled to temperatures beyond cold to soften and sculpt. Natural laen is black or smoky; can be cleared and tinted any transparent color. |
| Opal | Cloudy, rainbow-hued; favored by Elves for rings and weapon pommels. Fire Opal (red-blue-violet) highly prized; Black Opal rarer still and uniquely beautiful. |
| Ruby | Favorite stone of Noldorin smiths (used in Narya, one of the Three). Richest dark reds preferred by Elves; Men prefer brighter shades; Morgoth/Sauron's servants favor dark blood-reds or cloudy varieties. |
| Sapphire | Pure blue most prized by Elves (used in Vilya, one of the Three); yellow variety also valued. Black Sapphire very rare and ominous. |
| Violet Garnet | The only garnet taken seriously by the Noldor — clear pale violet, delicate and beautiful. Dwarves enjoy common dark reds; Men use most varieties, preferring violet; brown-green varieties favored in the south. Often found in large clumps of interlocking crystals. |

Difficulty of finding: Routine (+30) = 1; Easy (+20) = 2; Light (+10) = 3; Medium (0) = 4; Hard (–10) = 5; Very Hard (–20) = 6; Extremely Hard (–30) = 7; Sheer Folly (–50) = 8; Absurd (–70) = 9.

## 6.3 Metals

Steel and ithilnaur are the finest arms of Men, but Elves and Dwarves have access to metals with more remarkable properties. Some are drawn from veins within Endor's mountains; others are created only by combining minerals under conditions made possible by manipulating the Essence.

## 6.4 Stones

Quarried and used for building, statuary, and trinkets; frequently embedded with magic. The Drúadan Pûkel-men, Dwarven Watchers-in-stone, and the Daen Coentis Holy Tree are examples of Items of Power forged in stone.

| Name | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dirielong ("Gazing-rock") | Broad Dwarvish category for softer metamorphic and sedimentary stones used decoratively or where Naurond is unwieldy. Sub-types: "Block-stones" (colored marble and softer "Wet-rocks"/limestone), and "Slab-stones" (schist, flint, slate). Limestone cave formations — stalactites and stalagmites — are called "Aulë's Tears" by Dwarves and preserved wherever possible during delving. | — |
| Naurond ("Fire-rock") | General Dwarvish term for volcanic/igneous rock; the stone that binds Moria. Coarse granite near the surface; smooth black basalt deeper in. | Mirond ("Jewel-rock") | Dwarvish classification system for gems and glasses. Two principal categories: Fenen ("Veils" / "Hidden Crystals" / "Glasses") and Maegelenath ("Sharp-stars" / "Bold Crystals" / "Gemstones"). Of the glasses, laen is the most intriguing — as hard as diamond, smooth, without cleavage points, and colorable when worked. | Quartzes and obsidians provide most of Moria's glasses; no single Dwarven gemstone is considered preeminent. |
| Ulgond | Elven liquid stone poured into wood molds; hardens after a few days to a hardness exceeding any natural rock. Used extensively by the Gwaith-i-Mírdain. | — |

## 6.5 Woods

Bows, arrows, polearms, clubs, ships, cabinetry, and residences are crafted from wood. Creations of dírwood, teak, or oak can be imbued with magic as readily as those of steel, granite, or diamond.

|---|---|---|
| Black Cypress | Up to 200' tall; dark trunk, scale-like leaves. Grows throughout the Western Dominions of the Metharn. Produces some of the finest ship-building lumber in Middle-earth. Hard to work, but finished items are exceptionally strong (equivalent to –5 iron). | — |
| Blue Pine | Most common evergreen of the Rhudaur; bluish-silver wood. High tannic-acid content acts as a natural preservative — structures last twice as long as those of other woods. Large cones carry edible seeds (pine nuts). | Also listed under Herbs (Section 6.2) for its edible seeds. |

## 6.6 Enchanted Materials

Enchanted gems, metals, and stone may be formed in the forges of the mightiest Alchemists, but other sources of magical materials exist. Some are legend; others can be obtained from Endor's natural riches.

> Laen (see Gems and Stones), eog and kregora (see Metals), and ulgond (see Stones) are also enchanted materials and are described in their respective sections above.

| Name | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dragon Horn | Essentially unbreakable. Size and rib pattern signals a drake's power and denotes its stature in the species hierarchy. Prized for musical instruments and bow-making; can be ground to produce a host of enchanted potions. | — |
| Dragonskin | Highly valued as armor material; strength varies with the dragon's age (overlapping plates grow thicker over time). To harvest, an adventurer must contend with the dragon's toxic blood while cutting the scales and underlying hide. Making usable armor requires both an armourer and an alchemist. | — |
| Dragon Blood | Caustic and enchanted; difficult to store, transport, and utilize due to its corrosive properties. Can be made into potions granting the imbiber temporary ability to use mind-manipulating spells. Grows more powerful and more dangerously acidic as the dragon ages. | — |
