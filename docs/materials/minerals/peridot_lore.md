# Peridot -- Lore Research Entry

## Mineralogical Identity

**Name:** Peridot (pronounced PAIR-uh-doe or PAIR-uh-dot)
**Alt names:** Olivine (mineral name); Chrysolite (ancient/medieval -- any yellow-green stone; Greek *khrūsólithos*, "gold stone"); Topazos / Topazius (ancient Greek and Roman -- from the island source, not the modern topaz); Zabargad (Arabic, also the name of the source island); Pitdah (Hebrew -- contested identification); Faridat (Arabic root, possibly the origin of "peridot"); Evening Emerald (20th-century commercial name, now disused); Forsterite (most technically precise modern mineralogical name)
**Hardness:** 6.5-7 Mohs
**Crystal system:** Orthorhombic; nesosilicate; chemical formula (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄
**Color range:** Exclusively green -- from pale yellowish-green to deep olive; color from iron content in the crystal structure (idiochromatic -- color from its own chemistry, not trace impurities)
**Primary historic sources:** Zabargad (St. John's Island), Red Sea, Egypt; San Carlos Apache Reservation, Arizona (world's largest current supplier); Mogok, Myanmar; Pakistan (Kohistan, 1990s discovery); Norway; China

**Notable property:** Peridot retains its color under artificial light -- it does not darken at night. This is documented by Pliny the Elder and drove its historical nickname as the "Evening Emerald" and its association with the sun.

**Extraterrestrial occurrence:** Peridot occurs in pallasite meteorites -- stony-iron meteorites containing olivine crystals in a nickel-iron matrix, formed at the core-mantle boundary of asteroids 4.5 billion years ago. The Marjalahti pallasite (Russia, 1902 witnessed fall) was so high in quality that its olivine was adopted as the official gemological standard for peridot.

---

## Source Quality Summary

Peridot has excellent primary documentation from Egypt, Greece, and Rome -- all citing the same island source -- and well-documented medieval Catholic and Ottoman material records. The Hawaiian Pele tradition is genuine and rooted in the physical reality of volcanic olivine, though the specific antiquity of the peridot attribution is difficult to verify. The San Carlos Apache connection is material and economic rather than mythological. The biblical pitdah identification is well-argued by modern scholars but remains contested. There is almost no Tier 5-6 fabrication in peridot's history. Its main documentation issue is the opposite: ancient writers documented it under three different names (topazos, chrysolite, and later peridot) that only converged into the same stone for modern audiences in the 18th century, creating apparent gaps that are actually naming continuity problems.

## Geographical Survey

### Africa

**Egypt** | Tier 1-2

Zabargad Island in the Red Sea is the world's oldest documented gem olivine mine, with archaeological investigation (2010) confirming ancient mine workings. Mining likely began as early as 1500 BCE under the Pharaohs, worked through the Ptolemaic period and into the Roman era.

Ancient Egyptians worshipped peridot, referring to it as "the gems of the sun." Egyptian priests used goblets encrusted with peridot to commune with nature gods, believing the stone channeled the power of nature. Peridot was associated with the goddess Isis -- believed to protect its wearer from "terrors of the night," especially when set in gold.

Greek historian Agatharchides noted in *De Mare Erthraeo* that Egyptian kings ordered anyone who found these green stones to deliver them to the royal gem cutters for polishing -- a named primary source (2nd century BCE), Tier 1.

The Cleopatra connection: Most historians now accept that at least some of Cleopatra's celebrated emerald collection was in fact peridot from Zabargad. The two stones were systematically confused due to their similar color and shared Egyptian geographic source. This is a scholarly reassessment, not a myth.

After the 1270 CE attack on Aydhab by King David of Nubia, scholarly mentions of "zabargad" gems ceased. The violent disruption of the Red Sea port of Aydhab effectively ended peridot's supply to Europe for centuries, and the island's location was lost to European knowledge until its rediscovery around 1900.

**Sub-Saharan Africa, East Africa, West Africa, North Africa/Berber** | No documented tradition found.

### Middle East and Central Asia

**Arabia / Pre-Islamic** | Tier 1-2

The island of Zabargad sits in the Red Sea adjacent to both Egyptian and Arabian coasts. Ibn Hawqal, a Turkish traveler and geographer, specifically mentions peridot's Red Sea source in *Face of the Earth* (written 977 CE) -- a named primary source. The Arabic word *zabargad* became the dominant medieval name for the stone.

**Islamic / Ottoman** | Tier 2

The Ottoman Empire amassed the world's largest historical collection of peridots. Many Ottoman jewelry pieces, now exhibited at Topkapi Palace in Istanbul, feature peridots set in finely crafted gold. This is a documented institutional material record. No specific Islamic theological or hadith tradition for peridot comparable to carnelian's aqiq tradition was found.

**Mesopotamia, Persia/Zoroastrian** | No documented tradition found specific to peridot.

### Europe

**Greece and Rome** | Tier 1

Multiple named ancient authors documented the island of Topazos and its green stone: Agatharchides (2nd century BCE), Alexander Polyhistor, King Juba II of Mauretania, and Pliny the Elder (*Natural History*). Pliny documented that peridot glows like a hot coal at night, does not darken under artificial light, and is most powerful when worn on the right arm. Roman women of the nobility wore peridot; the Romans nicknamed it the "Evening Emerald."

A classical origin myth attaches to the island's discovery, transmitted through multiple authors: piratical troglodytes exhausted from storms came ashore on an island and discovered the stone.

**Biblical / Hebrew** | Tier 1-2 (with scholarly debate)

The Hebrew *pitdah* is the second stone in the first row of Aaron's High Priest breastplate (Exodus 28:17). Modern scholars broadly agree that pitdah refers to peridot: the island of Topazios (now Zabargad) is named in ancient literature; the Hebrew *pitdah* and Greek *topazios* both appear to reference gem olivine from this source; true topaz is far harder and more difficult to engrave; and geographic proximity of Zabargad to the Levant makes peridot accessible for the period. The tribe of Simeon was associated with the pitdah stone. The stone also appears in Revelation 21 and Ezekiel 28:13.

**Medieval Catholic / Crusader** | Tier 1-2

Crusaders returned peridot from the Holy Land to Europe in significant quantities. The most historically famous peridot artifacts in the world are three stones of over 200 carats each in the Shrine of the Three Holy Kings at Cologne Cathedral, completed 1225 -- for centuries believed to be emeralds, only correctly identified as peridot in the late 19th century by gemologist George Frederick Kunz. A bishop of Mainz wrote approximately 900 CE that "in the peridot is shown true spiritual preaching accompanied by miracles" -- a named primary ecclesiastical source.

**Napoleon / French Empire** | Tier 4

Napoleon gave peridot to the Empress Josephine as a token of devotion -- a named historical claim appearing in multiple Tier 4 jewelry history sources.

**Norse/Scandinavian, Celtic, Slavic** | No documented tradition found.

### The Americas

**Hawaiian (Polynesian)** | Tier 3

Locals refer to peridot as the "Hawaiian Diamond," and small peridot stones are sold as "Pele's tears" in honor of Pele, the goddess of volcanoes. In ancient Hawaiian chants, Pele was described as "She-who-shapes-the-sacred-land." The tradition has a compelling real-world basis: during some eruptions, lava carries bright green crystals to the surface, and olivine crystals wash ashore at Papakōlea Beach -- one of the world's only green sand beaches. The Pele tradition itself is ancient and well-documented in Hawaiian chants; the specific antiquity of the peridot-specific attribution is uncertain. Tier 3.

**San Carlos Apache (Southwest)** | Tier 2 (material/economic; mythology absent)

The San Carlos Apache Reservation hosts one of the world's most productive peridot localities -- Peridot Mesa. The stone is central to the tribe's artisan economy; one of the four reservation districts is named "Peridot" for the mesa. No documented San Carlos Apache mythological or ceremonial tradition specific to peridot was found.

**Mesoamerica, South America, New England Nations, Plains Nations** | No documented tradition found.

## Thematic Analysis

### Recurring Themes

**1. Sun, Light, and Protection Against Darkness** (Dominant -- Tier 1 in Egypt, Tier 1 in Rome, plausible in Hawaii)

This is peridot's single most coherent cross-cultural theme with a clear real-world basis: the stone's color does not change under artificial light. Pliny documented this as a fact. The Egyptians named it "gem of the sun." The Romans called it the Evening Emerald. The Hawaiian tradition connects it to Pele's volcanic fire. The protective-against-night-terrors use is a direct extension: darkness carries evil; a stone that retains its solar color in darkness therefore protects against nocturnal threats. This is one of the most internally logical connections between a stone's physical property and its attributed power in the entire history of gemstone lore.

**2. Confusion with Emerald** (Cross-cultural pattern -- Tier 1-2 across Egypt, Rome, medieval Europe)

Peridot's most historically significant characteristic is its continuous, documented misidentification as emerald across three millennia -- Cleopatra's collection, Aaron's breastplate, Cologne Cathedral, Ezekiel 28. The confusion is not random: large high-quality peridots from Zabargad do approach emerald green. This is the most famous case of gem misidentification in history, and that misidentification is itself a documented historical tradition. Peridot is a stone that spent most of its documented history wearing another stone's identity.

**3. Royalty, Sacred Use, and Priestly Function** (Strong -- Tier 1 in Egypt, biblical, Ottoman)

Egyptian kings monopolized peridot (Agatharchides, Tier 1). The stone appears on the High Priest's breastplate. Ottoman sultans accumulated the world's largest collection. Crusaders carried it as sacred spoil. In every era where it appears, peridot is associated with the highest levels of religious or political authority.

**4. Cosmic / Extraterrestrial Origin** (Modern -- Tier 2 scientific)

Peridot is the only stone in this project series with a verified extraterrestrial origin. Pallasite meteorites containing gem-quality olivine have fallen to Earth and yielded faceted peridots. The Egyptians believed peridot fell from the sky -- a belief now understood as observationally grounded, since olivine does literally fall from space. This is a myth that turned out to be partially accurate.

### Theme Coherence Assessment

The light/sun/protection-from-darkness cluster is extremely coherent and independently verifiable: the physical property (color retention under artificial light) is the obvious source of the attribution across all cultures. The Pele tradition is an outlier in tone -- the only peridot tradition rooted in grief and sorrow (the goddess's tears) rather than solar power and protection. The San Carlos Apache connection is an outlier in that it is purely economic. The dominant overarching theme: peridot is the stone of the sun preserved in matter -- light made mineral.

## Fictional Tradition

**Dungeons & Dragons:** Peridot appears in D&D gem tables as an ornamental stone in the 10-500 gp range. No distinct lore properties, dragon type, or named spell component role. Commodity stone, consistently placed below amethyst and carnelian in value hierarchy.

**Video games:** Peridot appears as a crafting ingredient in Elder Scrolls games and many other RPGs. In *Steven Universe* (animated television), Peridot is a named character associated with technology and analytical thinking -- a modern cultural identity distinct from its historical record, Tier 6 as attribution but with significant cultural reach.

**Lovecraft / Cthulhu Mythos, Tolkien / Middle-earth:** No documented connection found.

## LoreCodex Notes

Peridot has two design hooks with no equivalent in any other stone researched so far:

**The verified cosmic origin.** Peridot literally falls from space. The Egyptians believed it did -- and they were right. A material attributed with celestial properties whose extraterrestrial origin is scientifically confirmed is a uniquely defensible design choice. No fabrication required.

**The emerald confusion tradition.** A stone that spent three millennia being mistaken for another stone has obvious design potential in a system that distinguishes between magical materials by effect. Peridot and emerald sharing a visual identity with distinct magical properties is historically grounded rather than arbitrary.

The Pele tradition is the only volcanic origin myth for a gem with verified volcanic/impact origin. Peridot is literally born in lava -- again, the myth anticipated the science. This makes the Hawaiian tradition particularly rich for design rooted in elemental fire and volcanic themes.

**No Tier 6 fabrications identified.** The naming confusion is a genuine historical phenomenon. The Cleopatra peridot argument is a scholarly hypothesis, not a retail marketing claim.
