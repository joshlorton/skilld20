# Chauntea (The Great Mother, the Grain Goddess, the Golden Goddess, She Who Shapes All)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Chauntea
- **Titles/Epithets:** The Great Mother, the Grain Goddess, the Golden Goddess, She Who Shapes All
- **Portfolio:** Agriculture, plants cultivated by humans, farmers, gardeners, summer
- **Symbol:** A budding flower encircled by a sunburst (an older form of the symbol uses a sheaf of
  golden wheat on a green field instead)

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters, monastic orders
- **Other Clergy Grants:** Clerics and shamans are additionally encouraged (not required) to take
  herbalism, and receive agriculture as a bonus nonweapon proficiency.
- **Organization:** the church has two cooperative wings -- ordained clerics minister to the faithful
  in towns, cities, and civilized lands, while druids (who style themselves "True Clerics of
  Chauntea") work the outlying wild regions. Relations between the wings are cordial but sometimes
  strained, as the druids see the more recent city clergy as upstarts, while the clerics feel the
  druids' day is passing. Roughly 40% of the clergy are clerics and 50% are druids; mystics and
  shamans, who work alone and answer only to the goddess herself, make up 5% combined, and monks
  (always attached to a particular temple or druidic circle's leader) round out the last 5%. Most
  temples are small local shrines, often seed-storage caverns near pure wells, though a handful of
  large temples with granaries exist; services are also commonly held in open fields and druid
  groves.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Close One
  2. Watchful Brother / Watchful Sister of the Earth
  3. Trueseed
  4. Harvestmaster / Harvestmistress
  5. High Harvestmaster / High Harvestmistress
  6. Onum

### Dogma

- Growing and reaping, the eternal cycle, is the common thread of the faith.
- Destruction for its own sake, or leveling without rebuilding, is anathema to the church.
- Nurture, tend, and plant whenever and wherever possible.
- Protect trees and plants, and save their seeds so that what is destroyed can be replaced.
- See to the fertility of the earth, but let the human womb see to its own.
- Eschew fire.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Learn -- and pass on to fellow clergy and laity alike -- all they can of horticulture, herblore,
  plant types, and plant diseases.
- Encourage civilized folk to enrich the land through replanting, composting, and irrigation, rather
  than merely graze or dig it bare and move on.
- Replant trees, root out weeds that choke crop plants, and till plants back into the soil wherever
  they travel.
- Try to let no day pass without helping some living thing to flourish.
- Work against plant disease, sometimes hiring nonbelievers to burn diseased plants or plague-killed
  livestock, keeping careful watch over the resulting fires (priests otherwise dislike handling fire,
  though it is not forbidden to them).
- Tithe/offering: worshipers are expected to plant and faithfully tend at least one seed or cutting
  every tenday, till their own wastes back into the soil, and bring surplus seed to a temple for
  distribution to the less fortunate; coin given to a temple is said to return to the giver tenfold.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Daily Devotions | Daily, at dawn and dusk | Whispered thanks to Chauntea at the start of each day, and a prayer to the setting sun at day's end, from which the Great Mother is said to send her power. |
| Planting Prayer | Whenever planting occurs | Required at every planting; otherwise offered whenever worshipers are moved by nature's beauty. Best said on freshly tilled ground, farmland, or a garden, or failing that, at a well or watering place. |
| Wedding Night in the Field | Personal (weddings) | Newlyweds pass their wedding night in a freshly tilled field to help ensure fertility in the marriage. |
| Greengrass | Annual (spring fertility festival) | An uninhibited festival encouraging feasting, drink, and licentious behavior. |
| High Prayers of the Harvest | Local, timed to each community's actual harvest rather than fixed to Higharvestide | A solemn celebration of the harvest bounty Chauntea has given to a community. |

### Affiliated Orders

- No formal or informal affiliated orders mentioned -- the church has no dedicated military or
  knightly orders, and temples and shrines are guarded by members of the clergy themselves.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Druid (True Clerics of Chauntea, the self-styled collective title of the druidic
  wing)
- **Requirements:** Wisdom 12, Charisma 15
- **Prime Req:** Wisdom, Charisma
- **Alignment:** N
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Club, sickle, dart, spear, dagger, scimitar, sling, staff; favored
  weapons scythe & staff
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light armor only (padded, leather, or hide), plus a wooden, bone,
  shell, or other nonmetallic shield
- **Major Spheres:** All, Animal, Elemental, Healing, Plant, Protection, Time, Wards, Weather
- **Minor Spheres:** Divination, Travelers
- **Magical Items:** No limitations
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in agriculture, 1 rank in herbalism
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on survival (pick one terrain); +1
  bonus on handle animal (livestock and draft animals); bonus: choose two languages from brownie,
  dryad, elvish, korred, nymph, pegasus, pixie, satyr, sprite, sylph, treant, or unicorn

### Rank Progression (skill ranks 1-20)

Chauntea's specialty priests are simply members of the standard Druid class, and "their abilities and
restrictions... are summarized in Appendix 1: Priest Classes and detailed in full in the Player's
Handbook" -- i.e. they follow the ordinary Druid class progression, with no additional
Chauntea-specific bonus abilities beyond what's noted below.

| Rank | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | Shamans of Chauntea are immune to the poisons found in plants and mushrooms; clerics of Chauntea cannot draw spells from the elemental fire sphere (though they may access all other elemental spheres normally) |
