# Eldath (Goddess of Singing Waters, the Quiet One, Mother Guardian of Groves, the Green Goddess, the Mother of the Waters)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Eldath
- **Titles/Epithets:** Goddess of Singing Waters, the Quiet One, Mother Guardian of Groves, the Green Goddess, the Mother of the Waters
- **Portfolio:** Quiet places, springs, pools, stillness, peace, waterfalls, druid groves
- **Symbol:** A waterfall plunging into a still pool without causing any disturbance of its waters, or a circular, sky-blue disk fringed with green ferns

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters, monastic orders
- **All Clergy:** Required to take swim as a nonweapon proficiency, regardless of class.
- **Organization:** Small and dispersed, with most clergy traveling and preaching or settling
  quietly at some remote spot. A dozen or so priests, each with up to fourteen underpriests, report
  to a senior priest, who in turn reports to a grand priest responsible for a realm or larger
  region. Senior/high-ranking clergy are styled "Exalted," and it is from their ranks that the
  leaders (Keepers) of open-air temple communities called fastnesses are drawn. Only about 10% of
  Eldath's clergy are specialty priests (called peacemen and peacewomen), but relations among all
  five branches of the faith are excellent.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Underpriest
  2. (Full) Priest
  3. Freewalker (traveling adventurer-priest, ranks between full priest and senior priest)
  4. Senior Priest
  5. Exalted (title for clergy of senior years, many accomplishments, or high rank)
  6. Grand Priest (leader of a realm or larger region)
  7. Keeper of the Fastness (leader of an individual fastness temple community, drawn from the Exalted)

### Dogma

- Peace can only come from within; it cannot be taught or imposed, only reached through thought and meditation.
- The faithful should seek stillness, and thereby find peace.
- Plant trees and green-leaved things, and tend such things wherever needed.
- Nurture and aid; do not restrict or punish.
- Defend, but never punish; work violence only in defense.
- Slay no creature of the forest save to prevent it from slaying oneself or another under one's protection.
- Aid fellow Eldathyn and the clergy of Silvanus and Mielikki; shelter displaced forest dwellers and
  defenders of ponds, marshes, and streamside woods.
- Swear to take no thinking life save in direst need.
- Share the beneficial things that grow in or come from running water with all beings.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Tend unspoiled places: replant burned areas, purge plant diseases, build boulder firebreaks,
  irrigate wooded areas, clear streambeds, and build small dams to encourage pool life.
- Bargain with local foresters to limit cutting to certain areas and leave the rest alone.
- Work by whatever means seem prudent against indiscriminate woodcutters, those who use fire as a
  weapon, avaricious millers, and those who foul rivers and waters.
- Observe and record what birds, beasts, and plants dwell where, reporting changes to superiors so
  senior clergy (working with those of Silvanus and Mielikki) can track long-term ecological trends.
- Raise funds as water-dowsers (using an infallible water-divination spell known to the church), as
  herbalists, gardeners, or potion-makers.
- Teach swim to non-believers in exchange for small offerings of food or coin.
- Pray upon waking, at sunset, and at least once during the night; worship in wilderness areas is
  best performed unclad or as close to it as circumstances allow.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The Greening (Greengrass) | Greengrass (annual) | The only calendar-related ritual and the only regular large gathering/festival of the priesthood. |
| Firstflow | Varies by locale, as winter ice breaks up | A festival preceding the Greening, held whenever the local waters begin to flow again. |
| Consecration of a fastness | Whenever a new open-air temple site is dedicated | Assembled clergy perform the Chant of the Fastness; an avatar of Eldath always appears to bless the effort and may bestow healing power on the site's water for a tenday. |

### Affiliated Orders

- (Informal) Arbitrators of the Quiet One -- a circle of peacemen and peacewomen who travel to
  areas of conflict to mediate longstanding disputes without taking sides.
- (Formal) Disciples of the Yielding Way ("Brothers and Sisters of the Open Palm") -- the monastic
  order of Eldath; guards sacred sites and gathers information for isolated groves, deceptively
  deadly in self-defense despite never provoking violence.
- Close (informal) ties to the Shadoweirs of the Mielikkian faith (given sanctuary and supply) and
  to the Harpers ("Those Who Harp").

## Specialty Priests

Eldath has two specialty priest tracks: Peacemen/Peacewomen (the primary specialty-priest class, only
about 10% of the clergy) and Druids (roughly half of the clergy, the outlying-wilderness wing that
styles itself "True Clerics of Eldath"), who hold no Eldath-specific powers beyond the standard Druid
class.

### Peacemen / Peacewomen

- **Name/Title:** Peaceman / Peacewoman
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Nets, whips, staves, clubs, stones, slings, and staff slings only, under
  heavy restriction (see below); favored weapon net
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** None allowed
- **Major Spheres:** All, Astral, Charm, Divination, Elemental Water, Healing, Protection, Sun, Wards
- **Minor Spheres:** Animal, Necromantic, Plant, Travelers
- **Magical Items:** anathema items or effects that inflict harm on others
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in swim
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on linguistics (three woodland/fey
  languages); +1 bonus on stealth

### Rank Progression (skill ranks 1-20)

Mana formula: mana = 10 × spell level × uses per day. Non-daily rates normalize to a per-day
equivalent first.

| Rank | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pacifist restriction: may not fight except in defense of self or companions, nor initiate attacks, charges, or ambushes; those who bend this to their own ends lose all priestly abilities |
| 1 | May use any priest spell of the enchantment/charm type regardless of sphere, and may use Hold Plant despite it normally being a denied 4th-level minor-sphere spell |
| 1 | Foundation bonus: Remove Fear (10 mana), which also dispels magical fear effects (including Bane's fear aura) and the pain touch of Loviatar's priests |
| 1 | Immune to the pain touch of the priests of Loviatar |
| 1 | In combat, may parry: subtract Strength bonus + 1d6 directly from an opponent's attack roll; lost for the rest of the battle if the peaceman/peacewoman attacks |
| 3 | Foundation bonus: Sleep (10 mana per casting; one additional casting gained every 3 levels beyond 3rd) |
| 5 | Foundation bonus: Silence, 15' Radius (20 mana per casting; one additional casting gained every 3 levels beyond 5th) |
| 7 | Water Walk at will (no mana cost) |
| 10 | Can breathe water at all times (no mana cost) |
| 10 | Combat parry improves to Strength bonus + 1d10 |
| 12 | Foundation bonus: Laeral's Aqueous Column (40 mana) |
| 15 | Sanctuary in effect on self at all times; attackers must save vs. spell or be unable to target them |

### Druids

- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Club, sickle, dart, spear, dagger, scimitar, sling, staff; favored weapon
  net (per real-world published sources; not itself part of this track's book weapon list)
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light armor only (padded, leather, or hide), plus a wooden, bone,
  shell, or other nonmetallic shield
- **Major Spheres:** All, Animal, Creation, Elemental, Healing, Plant, Time, Wards, Weather
- **Minor Spheres:** Divination, Travelers
- **Magical Items:** No limitations
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in swim
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); bonus: choose two languages from brownie, dryad,
  elvish, korred, nereid, nixie, nymph, pegasus, pixie, satyr, sirine, sprite, sylph, treant, or
  unicorn

#### Rank Progression (skill ranks 1-20)

Eldath's druid specialty priests are simply members of the standard Druid class; per the source,
"their abilities and restrictions, aside from changes noted above, are summarized in Appendix 1:
Priest Classes and detailed in full in the Player's Handbook" -- i.e. they follow the ordinary Druid
class progression, with no additional Eldath-specific bonus abilities.
