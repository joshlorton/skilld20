# Vergadain (God of Wealth and Luck, the Merchant King, the Trickster, the Laughing Dwarf, the Short Father)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Vergadain
- **Titles/Epithets:** God of Wealth and Luck, the Merchant King, the Trickster, the Laughing Dwarf,
  the Short Father
- **Portfolio:** Wealth, luck, chance, nonevil thieves, entrepreneurial skills such as suspicion,
  trickery, negotiation, sly cleverness
- **Symbol:** A gold piece (always a circular coin); an older aspect of the symbol, tied to his identity
  as the Mulhorandi god Bes, is a dwarf wearing a panther skin and tail

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters, Rogues
- **All Clergy:** All clerics (including cleric/thieves) and specialty priests of Vergadain receive the
  standard dwarven clergy grants (literacy (Dethek runes), modified turn undead) -- see
  `dwarven-priests-general.md`.
- **Organization:** Vergadain's faithful are usually viewed as suspicious characters outside dwarven
  society -- respected for their commercial success but distrusted for their principles and practices
  -- so followers are permitted to deny their faith to outsiders without offending the god, so long as
  proper sacrifices continue. Temples are windowless chambers, either underground or in fortresslike,
  near-impregnable surface vaults, packed with coin and treasure rivaling a dragon's hoard and guarded
  by traps both magical and mundane; the central chapel is dominated by huge stone cauldron altars,
  each overhung by a 5-foot gold coin that functions as a guardian anator, blasting lightning bolts or
  magic missiles at anyone who removes an offering without whispering the correct password (known only
  to the temple's seniormost priest and to Vergadain himself). Novices are the Impoverished; full
  priests are Gilded Merchants. The clergy draws on gold dwarves (60%), shield dwarves (39%), gray
  dwarves (1%), and a handful of jungle dwarves, and is nearly evenly divided between specialty priests
  (37%), clerics (33%), and thieves (30%); priests were exclusively male before the Time of Troubles and
  remain 93% male today.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Impoverished (novice)
  2. Alloyn
  3. Copprak
  4. Argentic
  5. Electrol
  6. Aurak
  7. High Aurak (full priests collectively: Gilded Merchants)
  8. Merchant Princes (High Old Ones, who also hold individual titles)

  Specialty priests are known as hurndor (singular hurndar), a dwarvish word loosely translated as
  "those who trade."

### Dogma

- The truly blessed are those whose enterprise and zeal bring both wealth and good luck.
- Dwarves are well suited to earn their fortunes by the effort of both hand and mind; use both to pry
  wealth out of others.
- Work hard, be clever, seek the best bargain, and the Merchant King will shower you with gold.
- Live life to its fullest -- save, tithe, and spend your riches, and thus encourage more trade.
- Treat others with respect, but shirk not your responsibility to strike a deal better for you than for
  them; to not try would be to leave Vergadain's gifts idle.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Further dwarven merchant commerce with other races, especially humans, always to the benefit of the
  dwarves; the priesthood is expected to be personally wealthy and to maintain the god's temples in
  excellent style.
- Spend or trade gold donated on Vergadain's altars shrewdly -- bailing dwarven merchants out of debt,
  bribing officials to smooth dwarven trade with other lands and races, and otherwise increasing
  dwarven prosperity, often in cooperation with the clergies of Dumathoin and even Abbathor.
- Mutter Vergadain's name in homage whenever rendering aid or handling wealth of any sort; the god can
  see into the mind of any creature within 10 feet whenever his name is spoken this way, and sometimes
  warns a dwarf of treachery by vision or manifestation.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Coin Festivals (Trade Moots) | The days before and after a full moon, on Greengrass, and any day proclaimed holy by a Merchant Prince | The faithful seek to earn as much coin as possible beforehand so as to make garish displays of wealth and large tithes. Worship consists of slow, stately dancing around a flame in a windowless or underground room; every dwarf present throws in at least one gold piece, which the flame utterly consumes -- sometimes revealing a map, clue, scroll, potion, or (rarely) a weapon in its place. The dance ends when the flame flares upward in thanks; candles are lit, business is discussed, and funds change hands before the ranking priest passes a hand through the dying flame and every dwarf present kisses a gold coin in farewell. |

### Affiliated Orders

- *(Formal)* The Golden Hands of Vergadain -- a widely scattered order of priests and thieves found in
  most major dwarf-trading cities and along the major trade routes, who secure the safety of dwarven
  merchants and punish those who cheat the Stout Folk in exchange for a small percentage of any
  recovered wealth. In cities the order is structured like a thieves' guild, raiding warehouses of
  goods stolen from dwarven merchants; along trade routes it resembles a roving mercenary company of
  fighting clerics and specialty priests who destroy monsters and brigands, ransom kidnapped merchants,
  and recover plundered caravan goods.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Hurndor (singular hurndar)
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Club, dagger, dart, hand crossbow, knife, lasso, short bow, sling, broad
  sword, long sword, short sword, staff; favored weapons broadsword & longsword (Goldseeker)
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light (leather) or Medium (chain mail)
- **Major Spheres:** All, Astral, Charm, Creation, Divination, Guardian, Healing, Numbers, Travelers,
  Wards, Weather
- **Minor Spheres:** Animal, Combat, Protection, Sun, Time
- **Magical Items:** No limitations (same as clerics)
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in etiquette, 1 rank in gaming
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on appraisal; +1 bonus on linguistics
  (common, or a language used by a common trading partner of the dwarves); +1 bonus on literacy (common)
