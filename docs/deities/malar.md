# Malar (The Beastlord, Lord of Beasts, the Black-Blooded Pard, the Ravaging Bear)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Malar
- **Titles/Epithets:** The Beastlord, Lord of Beasts, the Black-Blooded Pard, the Ravaging Bear
- **Portfolio:** Hunters, marauding beasts and monsters, blood, bloodlust, evil lycanthropes, stalking
- **Symbol:** A brown-furred, bestial claw with long, curving talons tipped with fresh red blood

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** spellcasters, men-at-arms
- **All Clergy:** Required to take hunting as a nonweapon proficiency, regardless of class (animal
  lore and tracking strongly recommended); clerics of Malar are additionally permitted to use daggers.
- **Organization:** Loosely bound with no central hierarchy, organized around independent local cells
  called "Hunts." Each Hunt is led by a Huntmaster (priest, warrior, rarely a wizard, or a
  shapechanging predator), an office won and held by challenge -- a fight to the death if the
  incumbent won't resign. The Huntmaster decides the locale, time, and prey of the Hunt's ceremonial
  hunts.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy:** No formal ascending rank list. Priests of Malar are known
  collectively as Lords of the Hunt or Huntlords (to distinguish them from lay followers, who are
  merely "of the Hunt"). No individual titles are used except "Old Hunter," an address of respect for
  senior clergy; individual clergy are otherwise known informally by names such as "Brother Stag" or
  "Sister Wolf," earned by the most powerful beasts they've personally slain unarmed. Specialty
  priests of Malar are known as Talons.

### Dogma

- Survival of the fittest and the winnowing of the weak are Malar's legacy -- "May you die an old
  man" is a grave insult among Malarites.
- The hunt is the fulcrum of life and death, the true judgment of who may live or die.
- View every task as a hunt; remain ever alert and alive; walk the wilderness without trepidation and
  show no fear in the hunt.
- Savagery and strong emotion defeat reason and careful thought in all things.
- The strong must slay as often as possible and exult in the doing.
- Taste the blood of those you slay, and never kill from a distance.
- Tell the glory and danger of the hunt in grand tales to all who will listen.
- Work against woodcutters, farmers, and all who would cut back the forest and slay beasts merely
  because they are dangerous.
- Suffer no druid to live -- they preach a weak-minded balance that lets the inferior survive and
  often rule.
- Slay not pregnant wild creatures, young animals, or deepspawn, so that dire beasts remain plentiful
  to hunt.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Hunt as often as possible, routing the chase so its kill takes place in a settled area, publicly
  demonstrating the hunter's superiority.
- Preach the joys and bounty of the hunt; work to thwart the expansion of farms and settlements to
  preserve wilderness.
- Raid and vandalize the priesthoods of Chauntea, Deneir, Eldath, Silvanus, and Ilmater.
- Seek to slay druids of all faiths at every opportunity, seeing the natural Balance they promote as
  the true enemy of the hunt.
- Tithe/offering: no formal coin tithe -- offerings take the form of the hunt itself. The droning
  Bloodsong is intoned over the bodies of creatures slain in a hunt, and specific chants accompany any
  feast made from a hunted beast (sometimes a toast of the very blood of the animal killed).

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Feast of the Stags | Higharvestide (annual) | Malarite clergy parade through settled areas bearing the heads of beasts slain over the previous tenday, then lead a two-day feast open to all comers (even druids, under the protection of "the Peace of the Table"). Clergy publicly pledge to hunt through the coming winter for the tables of widows, the aged, the infirm, and orphans. This is the annual high point of public regard for Malar's faith. |
| High Hunt | Once each season (four times a year, per Malar's command) | A sporting event: all able clergy of a Hunt wear headpieces of beasts they've personally slain and wield only a single knife or the claws of Malar. A sentient humanoid quarry (never a Malarite, often a druid) is armed and released within a bounded wooded area. If the quarry escapes the bounds within a day and night, or survives until sunrise the following morning, they win permanent freedom and may ask one boon of the Huntmaster (short of killing a Malarite). Slain quarry are wholly burned as an offering to Malar. |

### Affiliated Orders

- No formal military or knightly orders.
- *(Informal)* Beastmasters -- lone, unaffiliated priests with an almost telepathic rapport with
  animals and predators, exerting druid-like control over wide swaths of wilderness through multiple
  concurrent find companion spells.
- *(Informal)* Beast Lords -- rare, lone spellcasters (mostly human, occasionally illithids, beholders,
  or other races) who breed unnatural monsters such as bulettes, stegocentipedes, owlbears, and
  perytons.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Talon (Talons of Malar)
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** All simple weapons (nonmissile), daggers, and the claws of Malar; favored weapon claw bracer (claws of Malar)
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Any (Light, Medium, or Heavy all permitted), plus shield
- **Major Spheres:** All, Animal, Combat, Healing, Plant, Summoning, Sun, War, Weather
- **Minor Spheres:** Divination, Elemental, Protection, Travelers
- **Magical Items:** No limitations
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in lore (nature)
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +2 bonus on survival (hunting); +1 bonus on
  survival (pick one terrain type); +1 bonus on stealth -- hunting is a survival skill action

### Rank Progression (skill ranks 1-20)

Mana formula: mana = 10 × spell level × uses per day. Non-daily rates normalize to a per-day
equivalent first (e.g. "2 per tenday" = 10 × level × (2/10) = level × 2). Where no spell level is
given but a class/character level is, use spell level = that class level ÷ 2, rounded up.

| Rank | Effect |
|---|---|
| 3 | Can identify plants, animals, and pure water with 98% accuracy |
| 3 | Foundation bonus: Beast Claw (20 mana) |
| 5 | Can track any animal by its spoor (grants a free rank in survival (tracking), or a +4 bonus if already trained) |
| 7 | Immune to charm spells or effects cast by woodland creatures |
| 10 | Foundation bonus: Rage (50 mana) *or* Animal Transfer (60 mana), choose one per use |
