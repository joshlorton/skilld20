# Halfling Pantheon -- General Notes

Shared context for the halfling pantheon (Yondalla's Children), drawn from the introductory prose to
the Halfling Pantheon chapter and from Appendix 1's discussion of halfling priests, in *Demihuman
Deities* (TSR 9585).

## Yondalla's Children

The Small Folk of the Realms worship a pantheon known collectively as Yondalla's Children -- a term
sometimes applied, by the tangled reasoning of halfling theologians, to all halflings collectively,
Yondalla included. The names by which the halfling gods and goddesses are known vary widely from
community to community, with little correlation to subrace. The myths of the various halfling powers
are often intermingled with tales of local halfling heroes and heroines of earlier generations who
embodied the teachings of one or more powers -- halfling villages scarcely two dozen miles apart
might each use a different name for Yondalla, believing the Protector and Provider to be a purely
local deity. The names used in the deity entries -- Arvoreen, Brandobaris, Cyrrollalee, Sheela
Peryroyl, Tymora, Urogalan, and Yondalla -- are simply the names by which the individual halfling
powers are most commonly known across the planes and by religious scholars of other races.

Yondalla is the universally acknowledged leader of the halfling pantheon; the other powers defer to
her authority without dissension, but in practice the entire pantheon works together collectively
for the good of the race, even dispatching avatars together as needed. The closest thing the Small
Folk have to an evil power they acknowledge is the gnome god Urdlen, the Crawler Below, said in some
tales to tunnel up into halfling burrows as well as gnome dens. The primary deities of the halflings
are mostly female, with the male gods seen as presiding over somewhat peripheral (if necessary)
aspects of life -- though all are equally respected. Divine coordination of portfolios is tightest
among Yondalla, Arvoreen, Cyrrollalee, Sheela Peryroyl, and Urogalan, with Brandobaris and Tymora
cooperating for the most part with each other. In some communities, two or three powers -- usually
Yondalla, Cyrrollalee, and/or Sheela Peryroyl -- are viewed as aspects of a single power.

Tymora is fully detailed in *Faiths & Avatars* (and, in this project, in her own existing file) since
she is most commonly worshiped by humans, not halflings. Although Lady Luck is in some sense an
interloper goddess in the halfling pantheon, the fragmented mythology of the Small Folk has allowed
her to be included as a local goddess under a wide variety of guises in halfling communities across
Faerûn -- tales of adventurous, lucky, tricky halfling damsels are long-standing halfling folklore,
and Tymora is commonly seen by the Small Folk as a long-standing local halfling deity who simply
conned the Big Folk into worshiping her as well.

## Halfling religious temperament

The typical halfling mentality holds that the only truly important things are those that happen
close to home. The Small Folk are far more interested in worshiping an immediate, beneficent deity --
one whose responsibilities are to them, and no one else -- than an abstract power presumed to oversee
the entire race. The remoteness of most human deities bewilders many halflings, as does the deference
human worshipers show their gods. Halflings are not irreligious; they treat Yondalla and her brood
with respect, but are far less in awe of their pantheon than is typical for the deity/follower
relationship of other races. As halflings see it, they have a simple bargain with their gods: in
return for their veneration, the powers promise to take care of all halflings. Halfling priests exist
to see that both sides of the bargain are kept -- to remind halflings to give the powers their due,
and to remind the halfling powers that they are responsible for the safety and comfort of their loyal
followers. Halfling folklore reminds the Small Folk that before they worshiped Yondalla and her
Children, halflings were a shy and fugitive people who lived as hunter-gatherers on the edges of
civilization, hiding in isolated burrows from the humanoids and monsters that preyed upon them; most
halflings today feel both gratitude and affection toward their deities as a result.

The Small Folk are also inclined to see evidence of small local deities -- the Small Gods or the
Thousand Home Gods of halfling folklore -- in many aspects of their surroundings and daily lives.
Each house commonly has a protector of its own hearth, often inspired by some matriarch or patriarch
in the clan's history; a homesteader who founds a small community might be accorded similar status in
later years, invoked on matters of the community's health and prosperity. Local myths may credit a
goddess of breadmaking for a particularly good batch of bread, or a local god of winemaking who
demands a toast after the first drink of an exquisite vintage. If game is plentiful, the power of the
neighboring woods (often pictured as a hare or fox) is thanked and given token offerings of food.
Halfling anglers commonly revere venerable river denizens, such as an ancient, battlescarred trout,
and always throw back the river deity if she or he allows itself to be caught. Some theologians of
other races speculate the Thousand Home Gods are simply aspects of the established halfling powers;
others say they are, at most, nature spirits. Halflings see little need to differentiate between
Yondalla's Children and the Small Gods and rarely bother to do so.

## Subraces and worship patterns

Halflings of all three subraces -- hairfeet, stouts, and tallfellows -- continue to venerate the same
core pantheon, albeit under a variety of names, and pay little attention to the differences between
the subraces in matters of worship. Scholars of other races have suggested stouts carry a trace of
dwarven ancestry and tallfellows a trace of elven ancestry, accounting for their close relations with
the Stout Folk and Fair Folk respectively (halfling slaves reportedly took refuge for generations
among dwarves beneath the Marching Mountains and among elves in the Darthiir Wood during the
Calishite diaspora). Overall racial distribution runs roughly 55% hairfoot, 30% stout, and 15%
tallfellow, and while some individual faiths draw a greater fraction of one subrace or another, the
variance is generally slight. The main practical difference in worship is that hairfeet more often
adopt an occasional human power (such as Tymora) into local practice, stouts sometimes give homage to
individual members of the Morndinsamman (the dwarven pantheon), and tallfellows sometimes give homage
to individual members of the Seldarine (the elven pantheon).

## Halfling priests (from Appendix 1: Demihuman Priests)

Halfling priests may be clerics, mystics, or specialty priests, and Sheela Peryroyl uniquely allows
druids as one of her two types of specialty priest (alongside greenfosters). Normally no multiclass
combinations are permitted to halfling priests, with two named exceptions: Arvoreen permits
fighter/clerics and fighter/specialty priests, and Brandobaris permits cleric/thieves and specialty
priest/thieves. Not all halfling powers permit mystics, though many do.

Every deity's clergy -- clerics, mystics, and specialty priests alike -- receives religion (halfling)
as a bonus nonweapon proficiency; this shows up in every deity file as the baseline "+1 bonus on
religion (pantheon)" entry for that deity's specialty priest.
