# Hathor (The Nurturing Mother, the Quiet One, the Dancer of Fortune, She Who Is There for Those in Need)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Hathor
- **Titles/Epithets:** The Nurturing Mother, the Quiet One, the Dancer of Fortune, She Who Is There for
  Those in Need
- **Portfolio:** Childbirth, motherhood, folk music, dance, poetry, the moon, fate
- **Symbol:** A horned cow's head wearing a lunar disk

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters
- **All Clergy:** All clerics, specialty priests, and mystics of Hathor receive religion (Mulhorandi),
  literacy (Mulhorandi), and linguistics (Common) as bonus skills, and all know Mulhorandi as their
  native tongue. All of Hathor's clergy must be human women of Mulhorandi extraction.
- **Organization:** Known as the Sisters of the Nurturing Mother, Hathor's clergy build no temples;
  funds they raise go directly to Mulhorand's poor and needy. Instead they maintain small roadside or
  village-center shrines, typically fountains adorned with dancing winged children and an image of a
  pregnant cow-headed woman, which are known to spontaneously flow with water or milk in times of need.
  Priestesses decline the honorifics "Lady Priestess" and "Lady High Priestess" that would technically
  be their due, preferring simply "Sister" or "Mother." About 65% of the clergy are specialty priests
  (revered mothers), 25% are clerics, and the remainder are wandering mystics who hold themselves apart
  from what little hierarchy exists. Hathor is venerated by the rural poor, mothers, and folk performers
  and artists; she is worshiped in the farmlands by serfs and slaves, in contrast to Nephthys, whose
  worship centers on the cities and rulers.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy:** Titles vary widely by temple, but Hathor's clergy commonly uses,
  in apparent ascending order (the source does not confirm this as a formal fixed hierarchy):
  1. Novice
  2. Daughter
  3. Sister
  4. Midwife
  5. Mother
  6. Favored Sister / Favored Midwife / Favored Mother
  7. Nurturing Sister

### Dogma

- Help all who turn to you for aid, be they slave or free, high born or low born -- all of Mulhorand's
  children are equal in Hathor's eyes.
- Provide sustenance where there is hunger, healing where there is hurt, health where there is disease,
  and protection where there is danger.
- Be humble; pride and haughty words do not come from the heart of the Nurturing Mother.
- Treat those in your care as royalty, for so shall you be cared for in the Quiet One's peaceful garden
  in the afterlife.
- Be joyful and spread happiness with your words and deeds; dance and let your heart be light.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Wander Mulhorand dispensing aid to the poor and downtrodden, nursing the sick, aiding the needy, and
  protecting children.
- Tithe nearly all personal income directly to those who need it most, living on the charity of those
  they aid.
- Show little interest in politics or temporal power -- a rarity among Mulhorand's priesthoods.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The Birthing | First full moon after Greengrass | Many priestesses give birth around this time; a time of great joy. Every priestess of Hathor not in the final stages of pregnancy or newly a mother is required to unveil a new song, dance, or poem. |
| Celebration of the Moon | Midsummer's Eve | A day of joyous revelry and uplifting hymns, often followed by moonlit trysts that account for the large number of newborns among the faithful the following year. |

### Affiliated Orders

- *(Informal)* Sisters of the Merciful Mother -- clergy of Hathor serving Mulhorand's cities who are
  sworn pacifists; their intervention on behalf of anyone injured or ill, even slaves, cannot be
  countermanded by anyone, not even a priest of Horus-Re.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Revered Mother
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Club, dagger, staff, flail, sling; favored weapon the sistrum mace -- an
  invented weapon, not found in the source or in any real-world D&D material, but modeled directly on
  a genuine historical precedent: the medieval "holy water sprinkler," a mace or flail nicknamed for
  its resemblance to a ritual aspergillum, which existed in both a spiked-mace form and a chained-flail
  form. Styled here as a light mace whose head is worked into the shape of Hathor's own sacred rattle,
  the sistrum -- reinforced and spiked for combat, still faintly jingling when swung -- this both
  supersedes the earlier D&D 3.5e citation (short sword, which conflicted with this book's own weapon
  list) and grounds the design in her single best-attested real-world attribute, since Hathor has no
  actual weapon in Egyptian mythology
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light and Medium armor (padded, leather, studded leather, or chain
  mail); shields allowed
- **Major Spheres:** All, Charm, Creation, Divination, Guardian, Healing, Protection, Summoning, Sun,
  Wards
- **Minor Spheres:** Animal, Combat, Elemental, Law, Necromantic, Travelers
- **Magical Items:** No limitations (same as clerics)
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in healing, 1 rank in performance (dancing)
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on herbalism; +1 bonus on performance
  (singing)
- **Other Clergy Grants:** Revered mothers who oversee a childbirth reduce the chance of a
  life-threatening complication for mother or child to 5% (a -5% penalty per child carried beyond the
  first in multiple births, floor 75%). Wealthy clients are expected to pay commensurate to their
  station (100 gp or more) for a revered mother's services; conversely, revered mothers must aid the
  poor, serfs, and slaves for free when called upon.
