# Gnome Priests -- General & Subrace Notes

Shared flavor for the Gnome Pantheon (the Lords of the Golden Hills) that applies across all nine
deity entries, drawn from the Gnome Pantheon's introductory prose and Appendix 1's "Gnome Priests"
discussion. Mechanical crunch specific to 2e classes (level caps, multiclass legality tables, exact
percentage-based racial ability chances) has been dropped; the flavor and setting context that
still matters for play has been kept.

## The Lords of the Golden Hills

The gnome pantheon is collectively called the Lords of the Golden Hills, named for the region of
Bytopia where most of them dwell. Garl Glittergold is clearly acknowledged as the pantheon's leader,
but he has never banished any god from it -- not even Urdlen, the Crawler Below, despite that evil
power's relentless, ongoing attacks against its fellow gods and against the Forgotten Folk. Urdlen
itself dwells in the Abyss but has repeatedly tunneled into Bytopia, only to be driven back each
time.

Several of the Lords of the Golden Hills travel with a boon companion: Arumdina, Garl's sentient
two-headed battle axe; Chiktikka Fastpaws, Baervan Wildwanderer's intelligent giant raccoon
companion; and an intelligent stone golem that accompanies Segojan Earthcaller. Other gnome deities
typically travel together or alongside a nongnome deity of related portfolio. This reflects a value
gnome myth places on companionship and sharing with trusted fellows, gnome or otherwise -- gnome
heroic tales almost always involve a faithful companion or significant aid from a well-disposed
deity or creature, often delivered as a hint or riddle.

Gnome deities themselves are said to have been born as gems or veins of rich ore in the heart of the
world, gradually released by the erosion of underground waters. Several are still linked in myth to
the gem or metal from which they were born: Baervan with emeralds, Callarduran with rubies, Flandal
with the legendary metal arandur, Garl with gold, and Segojan with diamonds. Some myths hold that
female gnome deities once existed; their fate (if they existed at all) has been lost, and the
all-male pantheon has nothing to say on the matter beyond a hint of ancient sadness. By tradition, a
young gnome who wishes to leave their community, even briefly, avoids the appearance of rejecting
family and friends by framing their wanderlust as a divine vision to search for the missing sister
gods.

Compared to other pantheons, the gnome gods dispatch avatars unusually often to intercede for their
worshipers -- but rather than appearing regularly to individual followers, they typically send
avatars on adventures that indirectly benefit the Forgotten Folk as a whole. A recurring, subtle
effect of these exploits, as gnome legends tell it, is that they encourage small groups of
worshipers to settle new lands they might not otherwise have explored.

Gnome folklore traces the origin of the first gnomes to gems Garl and Arumdina discovered in a
sealed, gem-studded cavern; when Garl polished and breathed on the gems, they opened like blossoms
to release the first gnomes, and Garl gave them laughter and mischief by way of a joke before
leading them into the world. Those born of diamonds became rock gnomes and chose to dwell beneath
the land; those born of emeralds became forest gnomes and chose to dwell amid great trees; those
born of rubies became the deep gnomes (svirfneblin) and wandered deep into the earth. Segojan is
predominantly identified with rock gnomes, Baervan with forest gnomes, and Callarduran with deep
gnomes, though each of the Lords of the Golden Hills is venerated across all three subraces.

Gnome history is poorly recorded, but a large population of the Forgotten Folk were enslaved by the
wizards of Netheril; the elves of Illefarn and Eaerlann sheltered small groups who escaped in their
frontier garrisons and taught them illusion-weaving so they could hide from recapture. A wave of
escapes and revolts led the Netherese arcanists to free their gnome slaves in -2387 DR, scattering
rock and forest gnome communities across the quiet backwaters of the Realms, chiefly south and east
of what is now Anauroch. It was in this same era of Netherese magical tinkering that the first
spriggans appeared.

Unlike the duergar and derro (who share mutual enmity with surface dwarves) or the drow (implacable
foes of surface elves), the deep gnomes entered the Underdark voluntarily and maintain good relations
with their surface kin.

## Deep Gnomes (Svirfneblin)

Deep gnome priests are gnome priests in every respect, but carry the abilities and weaknesses of
their subrace: they can innately duplicate minor illusion and disguise effects, radiate a
passive ward against divination, gain bonuses to avoid surprise, are naturally resistant to
illusions, phantasms, and hallucinations, carry meaningful innate resistance to hostile magic, and
(at higher levels, if not devoted to illusion magic themselves) can call on an earth elemental ally.
This mirrors and reinforces svirfneblin culture generally: a secretive, defensively-minded people
who prize misdirection and concealment as their basic survival tools, at ease with the ever-present
threat of the Underdark. Callarduran Smoothhands, patron of the deep gnomes, is himself
warden-figure and stone-lord to this subrace, and the priesthoods most closely tied to Underdark
survival -- Callarduran's earthbloods above all -- draw heavily from svirfneblin ranks.

## Forest Gnomes

Forest gnome priests are likewise gnome priests with the abilities and weaknesses of their subrace
layered on top: an affinity for the languages of treants, elves, and the simple speech of forest
animals (rather than the burrowing-animal ties more typical of rock gnomes); an innate knack for
passing unseen and hiding in woodland surroundings; heightened combat instincts against larger
opponents; and a particular readiness to strike back at orcs, lizardfolk, troglodytes, or any
creature they have personally seen damaging the forest. This subrace's woodland ties are most
directly expressed in the church of Baervan Wildwanderer, but the affinity colors how forest gnomes
practice any gnome faith -- Segojan's forest-gnome priests, for instance, are noted as leaning
toward their god's oversight of the natural world over his ties to earth and burrow.

## Spriggans

Spriggans are considered gnome priests structurally, but their racial legacy sets them apart: a
product of Netherese magical tinkering upon the enslaved gnomes of old Netheril, they can shift
between a diminutive and a giant-sized form at will and, in their small form, can innately spark or
smother flames, shatter brittle objects, and inspire supernatural fear. Most spriggan priests are
functionally priest/thieves. They are the only evil branch of gnomes in Faerûn, and while Urdlen has
adopted them as followers in part, the source material is explicit that they remain more of a
nuisance than a major threat, limited in number, and viewed by other gnomes chiefly as a cautionary
tale about what befalls those who give in to Urdlen's taint.
