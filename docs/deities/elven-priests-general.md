# Elven Priests (General)

This file collects pantheon-wide lore and shared clergy conventions for the Elven Pantheon (the
Seldarine) that aren't specific to any one deity -- drawn from the Elven Pantheon's own introductory
prose and from the "Elven priests" paragraph of Appendix 1, with pure 2e class-mechanical crunch
(multiclass combination tables, level caps) dropped per project standard. Individual deity files (see
`aerdrie-faenya.md`, `angharradh.md`, `avachel.md`, `corellon-larethian.md`, `deep-sashelas.md`,
`erevan-illesere.md`, `felarathael.md`, `fenmarel-mestarine.md`, `hanali-celanil.md`, and their
counterparts in the companion batch covering Khalreshaar through Zandilar) build on this shared
context.

## The Seldarine

- The Fair Folk of the Realms worship a pantheon called the Seldarine, roughly "the fellowship of
  brothers and sisters of the wood" -- a term implying both the wide diversity of interests among the
  elven gods and their desire for cooperation.
- The Seldarine act independently, drawn together by love, curiosity, and friendship to combine their
  strengths for a task or against outside threats, rather than by any central command. Corellon
  Larethian, the acknowledged ruler of the Seldarine (sometimes joined by his consort, identified as
  either Sehanine Moonbow or Angharradh depending on the myth-teller's subrace), compels none of the
  Seldarine to act; instead the gods simply sense when something needs doing and gather when necessary.
- With the sole exception of Fenmarel Mestarine, the Seldarine reside in Arvandor ("the high forest" in
  Elvish) on the plane of Arborea, on the layer known as Olympus.
- Formal membership in the Seldarine is determined by Corellon (or, per some myths, Corellon and
  Angharradh together). Unlike the dwarves, who still count the banished Laduguer among the
  Morndinsamman, the Fair Folk do not count banished members among the Seldarine.
- The good and neutral elven gods -- Corellon, Angharradh, Aerdrie Faenya, Deep Sashelas, Erevan
  Illesere, Fenmarel Mestarine, Hanali Celanil, Labelas Enoreth, Rillifane Rallathil, Sehanine Moonbow,
  Shevarash, and Solonor Thelandira -- have always been members in good standing, though Fenmarel has
  withdrawn from active involvement.
- Several drow powers -- Araushnee (now Lolth), Eilistraee, and Vhaeraun -- were once part of the
  Seldarine. They were exiled from Arvandor by Corellon's decree after the treacherous Araushnee and
  her complicit son assembled an "anti-Seldarine" coalition of evil gods that invaded Arvandor.
  Eilistraee did not intentionally participate in the scheme and willingly accepted banishment
  regardless, foreseeing a future need to guide drow who reject Lolth's dogma; she alone might someday
  formally rejoin the Seldarine, though she is more likely to simply remain a close ally.
- The Seldarine are closely linked to the gods of the Seelie Court (ruled by Titania and Oberon) and
  other sylvan deities; elven worshipers often include prayers to Seelie Court powers alongside the
  Seldarine, and elven faiths extend tolerance to closely allied nature cults such as that of Skerrit
  the Forester. The Seelie Court's enemy is the Unseelie Court, ruled by the Queen of Air and Darkness.
- The Seldarine take a very long view and rarely intervene directly in individual or small-group
  affairs. Notable exceptions include the creation and settlement of Evermeet, the Descent of the drow,
  the summoning of elven representatives to the Elven Court of Cormanthyr, the founding of Myth
  Drannor, the creation of the Harpers, the Retreat (beginning in the Year of Moonfall, 1344 DR), and
  the defense of Evermeet (Year of the Unstrung Harp, 1371 DR).

## Origins and the Tel'Quessir

- Elven mythology holds that the Fair Folk were born of blood Corellon shed battling Gruumsh, mingled
  with the tears of Sehanine (or Angharradh). Most Seldarine members dislike or distrust the goblinkin
  pantheons (orcs, goblins, hobgoblins, bugbears, kobolds, urds) as a result. Alternate legends hold the
  first elves appeared fully formed in Corellon's image, woven by magic from sunbeams, moonbeams,
  forests, clouds, seas, and shadows; others claim some subraces (notably gold and moon elves) migrated
  to Abeir-Toril through magical gates from another world, commonly called "Faerie."
- Several Seldarine members can assume a nonelven natural form far larger than their usual avatar:
  Rillifane Rallathil as a massive oak, Deep Sashelas as a towering humaniform wave, Aerdrie Faenya as a
  white cloud, and Corellon Larethian as an azure crescent moon or star.
- Elven theology closely ties the Fair Folk to magic and the natural world -- elves have spirits, not
  souls, and many believe they will be reincarnated as animals, plants, faerie folk, or elves again.
  Elves are creatures of the Weave, tightly bound to Abeir-Toril's web of magic.
- The Fair Folk call themselves Tel'Quessir ("the people"); all other beings are N'Tel'Quess
  ("not-people"). The historical subraces are the Sy-Tel'Quessir (green/forest/sylvan/wood elves), the
  Ly'Tel'Quessir (lythari), the avariel (winged elves), the Ssri-Tel'Quessir (dark elves/Ilythiiri), the
  Ar-Tel'Quessir (gold/sun/sunrise/high elves), the Teu-Tel'Quessir (moon/silver/gray elves), and the
  Alu-Tel'Quessir (aquatic/sea/water elves). All have interbred with humans to form half-elves; only in
  Deepingdale, Loudwater, Dambrath, and the Yuirwood are half-elf populations even relatively stable.
  Elven churches -- particularly Hanali Celanil's -- are far more welcoming of half-elves than elven
  society at large.
- The Crown Wars, a series of five conflicts driven by the expansionist Vyshaantar Empire and the
  cruelty of Ilythiir, shattered early elven civilization. After the fourth Crown War, the Seldarine
  transformed the surviving Ssri-Tel'Quessir of Ilythiir into the obsidian-skinned, white-haired
  drow ("dhaera," an elvish term for traitor) and banished them to the Underdark -- the Descent. The
  Vyshaan were later found culpable at the Elven Court and their empire destroyed in the fifth Crown
  War.
- Individual elven powers are closely tied to specific subraces: Corellon Larethian and Hanali Celanil
  to the Ar-Tel'Quessir (gold elves); Hanali Celanil and Sehanine Moonbow to the Teu-Tel'Quessir (moon
  elves); Deep Sashelas to the Alu-Tel'Quessir (sea elves). Before the Descent, the Ssri-Tel'Quessir
  were tied to Araushnee (now Lolth), Eilistraee, and Vhaeraun -- a relationship that persists between
  the drow and the dark gods they still worship.
- In the Yuirwood, the Sy-Tel'Quessir once absorbed a small pantheon of primitive gods venerated there
  before them (the "gods of the Yuir"); nearly all were subsumed as wild, primitive aspects of Rillifane
  Rallathil, Shevarash, Labelas Enoreth, and other Seldarine members. Only Zandilar the Dancer retained
  any independence, and she later merged with the Mulhorandi goddess Bast (Sharess) after a failed
  gambit against Vhaeraun.

## Shared Clergy Conventions

- Per Appendix 1, elven priests are drawn from the classes of cleric, crusader, druid, mystic, and
  specialty priest (not every class is available to every deity's clergy -- see each individual deity
  file for which classes actually appear in its church).
- Nearly universally, a deity's core clergy classes (typically clerics, including multiclassed
  half-elven clerics, plus whichever of crusaders/druids/mystics/specialty priests that deity's church
  includes) receive religion (elf) and literacy (Espruar) as bonus nonweapon proficiencies -- distinct
  from, and in addition to, the baseline "+1 religion (pantheon)" Skill Bonus given to each deity's
  specialty priest track in these files. Several deities extend or vary this baseline (for example,
  Deep Sashelas grants religion (elf, aquatic) instead, and Aerdrie Faenya's clergy additionally
  develops a fear of confinement); see each deity's own "All Clergy" entry for specifics.
