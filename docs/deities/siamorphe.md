# Siamorphe (the Noble, the Divine Light, la Noblesse Oblige)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Siamorphe
- **Titles/Epithets:** the Noble, the Divine Light, la Noblesse Oblige
- **Portfolio:** Nobles, nobility, rightful rule of nobility, human royalty
- **Symbol:** A silver chalice with a golden sun on the side of the cup, or a circlet with a golden sun
  on the brow

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters (clerics, specialty priests, paladins, wizards), Men-at-arms (fighters,
  paladins)
- **All Clergy:** All clerics and specialty priests of Siamorphe receive religion (Faerûnian) as a
  bonus skill.
- **Organization:** The clergy is collectively known as the Scions of Siamorphe, so named because most
  are directly descended from the mortal form of the goddess (or one of her predecessors) and
  theoretically could inherit the mantle of divinity themselves. Most members of the clergy are titled
  nobility. The breakdown among classes runs roughly 5% fighters, 10% paladins, 10% wizards, 40%
  clerics, and 35% specialty priests (known as highborns). Siamorphe is little known outside the
  nobility, and even less known by nobles outside Waterdeep, where the charity of the historical Lady
  Siamorphe still lives on in the tales of bards. The only true temple remaining in the Realms, the
  Chapel and Chalice of the Divine Right, stands in Waterdeep; a few minor chapels have recently been
  refurbished in Tethyr, and long-neglected shrines still stand in Cormyr and Baldur's Gate on the
  estates of various noble families. Temples are small chapels typically accessed from a lord's castle
  hall, built with marble floors, mahogany paneling, and stained-glass windows, hung with the sponsoring
  noble family's crest above the altar.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Lord/Lady Scion
  2. Baron/Baroness Scion
  3. Viscount/Viscountess Scion
  4. Count/Countess Scion
  5. Marquis/Marquise Scion
  6. Duke/Duchess Scion
  7. High Lord/High Lady Scion

### Dogma

- Nobles are the rightful rulers of humankind, provided they keep themselves fit for the task and
  responsibilities of rule.
- Nobles bear a moral obligation to rule in the best interest of those who serve under them, even when
  it conflicts with personal desire.
- Noble bloodlines inherit charisma and the potential for wisdom, and their family fortunes buy the
  leisure to be properly educated as rulers.
- Strict, regular inheritance of noble titles reduces the potential for power struggles between rival
  claimants to leadership.
- A noble class that cares for the commoners under it and looks to their best interests is the most
  stable, fair form of government yet devised.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Serve as advisers and councilors to noble rulers, or rule directly if of noble birth.
- When a noble ruler proves unfit for the task, work to rectify their shortcomings through counseling
  and education, or engineer their succession by a more fit ruler of noble lineage.
- Preside over advisory councils, research the genealogies and histories of noble families, coordinate
  ceremonies of investiture, and determine lines of succession.
- Provide advice, arbitrate civil disputes, give leaderless folk direction, and preach the mutual
  rights and responsibilities of nobility and commoners.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Divine Pageantry | Summer, on a date set by Siamorphe's will | In Waterdeep's Sea Ward and North Ward, nobles take over Heroes' Walk, Heroes' Garden, and the road to the Street of the Singing Dolphin, donning archaic costume, throwing coins to commoners, and speaking in centuries-obsolete formal address. |
| Ceremony of Divine Investiture | At a noble heir's birth, a coronation, or any investiture of title | A baptismal-style rite performed via the divine investiture spell; among Waterdeep's nobility, such a ceremony can cost as much as 10,000 gp. |

### Affiliated Orders

- (Formal) The Knights of the Silver Chalice -- founded in Tethyr by Marquise Scion Aalangama
  Gulderhorn after the Time of Troubles, composed primarily of noble-blooded warriors who escaped the
  Ten Black Days of Eleint; work to find survivors of the civil war who will govern justly and restore
  them to positions of power.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Highborn
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** All bludgeoning (wholly Type B) weapons; favored weapon light mace (per
  this book's own avatar-wielded scepter, Noble Might, and matching the real-world 3.5e citation of
  "Noble Might [scepter], a light mace")
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Medium and Heavy (chain mail or better metallic armor), plus shield
- **Major Spheres:** All, Astral, Charm, Divination, Healing, Law, Sun, Thought, Time
- **Minor Spheres:** Combat, Creation, Guardian, Numbers, Protection, War, Wards
- **Magical Items:** No limitations (same as clerics)
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in handle animal (horse), 1 rank in literacy (Common or native tongue,
  pick one), 1 rank in linguistics (pick one human or demihuman tongue)
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on appraisal; +1 bonus on etiquette; +1
  bonus on heraldry; +1 bonus on literacy (pick one)

Highborns must be legitimate descendants of long-established noble families (titled for at least three
generations, most from Waterdeep though some from Tethyr or Cormyr) and must be human or at least half
human. They are also skilled in fashion: they always know the current modes of dress among nobility and
commoners alike in any place they know at least passingly (needing 2d20 days to learn the fashions of
an unfamiliar location), checked as a Wisdom ability check with a +1 bonus.
