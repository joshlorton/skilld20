# Drow Pantheon -- General Notes

Shared background and mechanics for the six drow deities (Eilistraee, Ghaunadaur, Kiaransalee, Lolth,
Selvetarm, Vhaeraun) that don't belong to any single deity's own entry. Deity-specific restrictions
(such as Eilistraee's and Lolth's clergy needing to be female, or Vhaeraun's needing to be male) stay
in each deity's own "All Clergy" field rather than here.

## Origins & Family Ties

- The drow -- the Ssri-Tel'Quessir, or "dark elves" -- are a subrace of elves long divorced from the
  Seldarine, the traditional elven pantheon of Arvandor. Their own pantheon is a pantheon in name
  only: its members are united chiefly by their worshipers' common heritage, old family ties among
  four of them, and occasional, short-lived alliances of convenience.
- By Corellon Larethian's decree, the destiny of the dark elves was placed in the hands of his
  consort, Araushnee the Weaver -- at the time a minor but secretly ambitious member of the Seldarine.
  After a series of betrayals (aiding Gruumsh One-Eye against Corellon, then setting Malar on his
  trail) and after secretly raising a host of evil deities -- the "anti-Seldarine" -- to assault
  Arvandor and replace Corellon as its ruler, Araushnee was transformed into a spider-shaped tanar'ri
  and banished to the Abyss. There she took the name Lolth, Demon Queen of Spiders, conquered a
  portion of the Abyss, and drove off or subjugated rivals such as Ghaunadaur and Kiaransalee.
- Araushnee's own children were swept up in her fall. Vhaeraun, her son by Corellon, was banished for
  his complicity in her plots. Eilistraee, her daughter, was only an unwitting participant but chose
  exile alongside her mother and brother anyway, foreseeing a future need to balance their evil.
  Vhaeraun and Eilistraee are thus brother and sister.
- Selvetarm is the offspring of an ill-fated tryst between Vhaeraun and Zandilar the Dancer, a
  goddess once venerated by the Yuir elves; Zandilar later merged her weakened essence with the
  Mulhorandi goddess Bast to become Sharess.
- Ghaunadaur is a primordial evil unrelated to Araushnee's family, who simply joined the anti-Seldarine
  host in its assault on Arvandor.
- Kiaransalee is a once-mortal dark elf of another world (Threnody) who achieved divinity and was
  named drow in her own right, before Araushnee's fall.
- Lolth dominates the pantheon and brooks (or at least admits) no challenge to her authority. Only
  Kiaransalee and Selvetarm formally acknowledge her as head of the pantheon -- an unavoidable
  acknowledgment of her raw power. Eilistraee, Vhaeraun, and Ghaunadaur remain independent of Lolth's
  control, but none is strong enough to challenge her directly, and their mutual enmity precludes any
  alliance against her.

## Drow Worship & Culture

- Drow religion is a curious mix of monotheism and polytheism uncharacteristic of most other
  Faerûnian cultures. Most drow cities are ruled in the name of a single patron deity (Lolth, in most
  cases, though a few cities are ruled in the name of Ghaunadaur or Kiaransalee instead), and even
  mentioning -- let alone worshiping -- any other god is forbidden. The rare drow cities that openly
  tolerate worship of two or more deities are invariably riven by strife, and are usually destroyed by
  civil war within a generation of the split appearing.
- Even so, most of the drow gods retain a few secretive worshipers in every drow enclave, since such
  hidden devotion gives dissidents an extra weapon in their endless quest for personal advancement.
  Aside from the faithful of Eilistraee, who venerate the Dark Maiden in a manner resembling how
  surface elves venerate the Seldarine, most drow venerate their patron out of fear, respect, and a
  desire for power -- not out of any real piety.
- Over 10,000 years ago, the Crown Wars exposed how deeply the dark elves had fallen into the service
  of Vhaeraun, Ghaunadaur, and Lolth, despite Eilistraee's efforts to hold them back. The Seldarine and
  the allied elven nations banished the dark elves to the tunnels beneath Faerûn, thereafter naming
  them drow (from *dhaeraow*, an elvish term meaning "face of shadow, heart of night, traitor").
- The first drow kingdom, Telantiwar, was seated in the great cavern of Bhaerynden, seized from the
  Stout Folk. It tore itself apart in civil war between rival nobles and priests, and the cavern's roof
  collapsed to form what is now the Great Rift. In the resulting diaspora, the Scattering, the
  surviving drow nobles and priests fled into the Underdark with what people, slaves, and equipment
  they could seize, founding the scattered drow cities and settlements that exist across the Underdark
  today.

## General Drow Priest Abilities

- Drow priests may be clerics, crusaders, druids, mystics, or specialty priests, though no member of
  the drow pantheon openly accepts druids into their service -- the only drow druids are typically
  neutral dark elves who have renounced the Underdark and joined a surface Faerûnian human faith
  instead. Drow cannot become shamans.
- Clerics, crusaders, and specialty priests of every drow deity (the exact class list varies by
  deity -- see each deity's own "All Clergy" field) receive religion (drow), religion (elven), and
  reading/writing (drowic) as bonus proficiencies from their patron, on top of any further
  deity-specific proficiencies (such as Lolth's ancient languages (high drow)) noted in that deity's
  own entry.
- All drow, priest or otherwise, know linguistics (low drow, equivalent to Undercommon) and
  linguistics (drow silent speech) as innate abilities.
- All adult drow have an innate set of base spell-like powers usable once per day -- dancing lights,
  faerie fire, and darkness -- and, from 4th level on, a set of mature powers also usable once per day
  -- levitate, know alignment, and detect magic. Both sets of powers fade the longer a drow spends away
  from the Underdark, on the surface world.
- Beyond their normal spells, drow priests receive additional spell-like powers through their deity's
  divine favor that do *not* fade with time on the surface, typically including clairvoyance, detect
  lie, suggestion, and dispel magic (sometimes extended to detect undead, ESP against other drow at
  short range, or invisibility to undead). These powers can improve or expand as a priest gains levels.
- Drow of noble blood or name typically gain the ability to use their base, mature, and priestly
  powers more than once a day, the eldest and most accomplished among them the most. Especially able,
  experienced drow can combine a natural spell-like power with a spell, or use two natural powers,
  simultaneously.
- Strong light hampers drow concentration: noble drow and drow priests risk faltering when they try to
  launch a new spell or spell-like power while in strong light (already-active effects are
  unaffected), and all drow are limited to using only one spell or spell-like power at a time under
  such conditions.
- All adult drow, including drow priests, carry some degree of innate resistance to magic that grows
  stronger with experience, along with an innate bonus on saving throws against magical attacks
  generally. Like the base and mature powers above, both fade the longer a drow spends away from the
  Underdark.
