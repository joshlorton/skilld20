# Gargauth (The Outcast, the Lord Who Watches, the Tenth Lord of the Nine, the Lost Lord of the Pit, the Hidden Lord)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Gargauth
- **Titles/Epithets:** The Outcast, the Lord Who Watches, the Tenth Lord of the Nine, the Lost Lord of
  the Pit, the Hidden Lord
- **Portfolio:** Betrayal, cruelty, political corruption, ill counsel, self-serving advisers, political
  puppetmasters, powerbrokers, powermongers
- **Symbol:** A broken animal horn

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters
- **All Clergy:** All clerics and specialty priests of Gargauth receive religion (Faerûnian) as a bonus
  proficiency.
- **Organization:** A cast-out devil rather than a native Faerûnian power, Gargauth was exiled from his
  palace in Baator and now wanders the Prime Material Plane; he became a demipower by destroying and
  absorbing the essence of other baatezu physically present in the Realms. Few beings even know he
  exists, and those who do rarely speak his name for fear he will pay them a visit. His clergy is split
  evenly between clerics and specialty priests (malefactors), with the balance slowly shifting toward the
  latter, whom Gargauth favors. All clergy are organized into a strict hierarchy with corresponding
  titles, and higher-ranking priests often adopt an additional personal title referencing a variant of
  the true name of some baatezu Gargauth has destroyed. Gargauth has very few temples; those that exist
  are typically built beneath large cities and reached by a deep pit, their walls lined with scenes of
  Baator, their priests masked to resemble various baatezu, and their sanctuaries dominated by huge
  brazen altars amid sulfurous incense and fire pits.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Supplicant (novice)
  2. Lord of the First Pit
  3. Lord of the Second Pit
  4. Lord of the Third Pit
  5. Lord of the Fourth Pit
  6. Lord of the Fifth Pit
  7. Lord of the Sixth Pit
  8. Lord of the Seventh Pit
  9. Lord of the Eighth Pit
  10. Lord of the Ninth Pit (9th level and higher; higher-ranking priests typically add an individual
      title as well)

### Dogma

- Life is the accumulation of power. Civilization is a thin veneer over the base desires at the core of
  every living being; those who wish to survive and prosper must recognize this and focus all their
  resources on the pursuit of power.
- High moral principles are hypocrisy -- all beings act in their own self-interest, some merely cloaking
  it in sanctimonious philosophy. Use charm and honeyed words, or a barbed and bloody dagger, as the
  situation demands.
- It is more important to rule than to sit on the throne; often the being behind the ruler holds the true
  power.
- All beings are regimented in a strict hierarchy governed by the politics of power; those who avoid the
  rules of the game are ground beneath the heels of the powerful.
- Keep to the letter of any agreement and any rule set by the more powerful, but be prepared to twist any
  contract or stricture to maximize the benefit you receive.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Keep the faith secret while working to increase personal power, the power of the church, and, by
  extension, the power of Gargauth.
- Serve as Gargauth's eyes and ears throughout the Realms; entice and corrupt powerful individuals and
  community leaders, particularly through the careful application of spiritual corruption, and bind them
  into strict contracts favorable to Gargauth's goals.
- Seize positions of power for oneself whenever possible and weave them into the faith's secretive
  hierarchy.
- Focus effort on undermining rival powers and faiths -- currently Iyachtu Xvim, Cyric, Shar, and
  Loviatar -- to draw like-minded converts, rather than spending strength against opposed faiths such as
  Lathander or Tyr.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The Unveiling | Midwinter night | A horrific ceremony believed to involve gruesome sacrifices, heralding the long-anticipated day Gargauth seizes the Realms and transports all of Faerûn to Baator as a new layer. |
| The Binding | Eve of the Feast of the Moon | A personal ritual in which each priest renews their eternal contract with Gargauth, trading absolute fealty for increased power; believed to involve sacrifices of money, magic, and hoarded knowledge alongside variant castings of spiritual corruption and mask of Gargauth. |

### Affiliated Orders

- *(Informal)* A handful of secretive cults, fellowships, and forgotten wizards' cabals scattered
  throughout the Realms, most of whose members -- and even leaders -- do not know the true identity of
  their divine patron.
- *(Informal)* The Knights of the Shield -- a network of merchants, nobles, and power brokers spanning
  Amn, Tethyr, Calimshan, Baldur's Gate, and the trading cities of the Western Heartlands and Dragon
  Coast, working to manipulate the politics and money of the Sword Coast. Secretly ruled by the Shield
  Council, with Gargauth as its undisclosed "Hidden Lord of the Shield," represented day-to-day by the
  priest Ghantuz the Cloaked as Second Lord.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Malefactor
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Any; favored weapon dagger ("Corruptor" per real-world 3.5e sourcing,
  matching Gargauth's own signature throwing daggers +3 and the malefactor's required proficiency in
  throwing dagger)
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light, Medium, and Heavy (any armor)
- **Major Spheres:** All, Astral, Charm, Combat, Law, Necromantic, Protection, Thought, Travelers
- **Minor Spheres:** Creation, Divination, Healing, Numbers, Sun, Time
- **Magical Items:** No limitations (same as clerics)
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in throwing dagger (weapon skill), 1 rank in etiquette, 1 rank in literacy
  (Common)
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on literacy (Thorass); +1 bonus on
  linguistics (pick any three)
