# Garagos (The Reaver, Master of All Weapons, Lord of War)

## Deity Info

- **Name:** Garagos
- **Titles/Epithets:** The Reaver, Master of All Weapons, Lord of War
- **Portfolio:** War, skill-at-arms, destruction, plunder
- **Symbol:** A five-armed tentacus -- a pinwheel of five black, snaky arms spinning counterclockwise,
  each arm ending in an identical sword

## Church Summary

- **Clergy:** Spellcasters, Men-at-arms (crusaders)
- **All Clergy:** All clerics, specialty priests, crusaders, and shamans of Garagos receive religion
  (Faerûnian) as a bonus proficiency.
- **Organization:** The Garagathan faith is not organized as a whole -- it exists as a patchwork of
  independent churches with their own hierarchies, several of which actively feud. Two rival churches
  contend for dominance in Amn and Tethyr; another in the Sword Coast North seeks to expand its sway over
  the whole Sword Coast and into Cormyr. The oldest church, in Westgate, has always been split into
  warring cults, presently dominated by a Yondath-based clergy contested by rival organizations in the
  Great Dale (pushing into Damara and Thay) and in Raurin (spreading agents south and west). A newly
  rebuilt temple at Gosra has drawn representatives from every established church to help form its
  hierarchy -- mostly by infighting over which church it should ultimately belong to, while also
  scrambling to defend it against a rumored mercenary army hired by the church of Tempus. Overall the
  clergy is roughly 45% clerics, 40% specialty priests, 10% crusaders, and 5% shamans; shamans appear
  only in more primitive, berserk-raiding cultures and are a dwindling presence as Tempus's worship has
  displaced Garagos's in most organized settings. Clergy address one another as "Bloodbrother" or
  "Bloodsister," adding "High" when speaking to a priest four or more levels senior; formal titles beyond
  the general ranks below are rare, though senior priests often append a self-styled honorific (e.g.
  "Favored High Reaver Ounadar the Blood-Drenched"). Priests attached to a military force may also hold
  rank within it.
- **Ranks/titles within the clergy** (ascending order):
  1. Supplicant (novice)
  2. Priest/Priestess of the Blood (full priest)
  3. Reaver Lord/Lady (senior priest)
  4. Favored (veteran senior clergy of ruling rank; combined with a self-chosen honorific)

### Dogma

- Peace is for weak fools; war makes all who fight strong, and only head-to-head conflict satisfies
  honor. Only cowards avoid battle.
- Any who strike down a foe from ambush or from behind are scorned as cowards.
- Retreat is never an option, even against a greater foe -- a warrior focused on Garagos will be given
  the strength to conquer any enemy.
- Diplomacy is for fools, the soft, and the dishonorable; the only true answer lies in battle.
- A warrior's word is a bond to a friend, and no one is more trustworthy than a shield companion, but no
  pledge is owed to a cowardly or enemy dog.
- Battlelust is a gift from Garagos; it grants the faithful focus and strength, and refuge from the
  confusion and pain of the battlefield.
- Bow down to Garagos and triumph in arms; awaken bloodlust and reaving wherever prudent or possible.
  Go armed at all times. Do battle at least once a tenday and shed blood even where you cannot slay.
  Once a year, challenge and slay a foe greater than yourself -- usually interpreted as a powerful
  monster or a higher-level priest of another faith.

### Day-to-Day Activities

- Foment strife wherever they go, seeking to cause battle so bloodlust (the Sacred Goal) is born and
  wanton destruction begins.
- Some clergy work as sly manipulators dealing in intrigue, thievery, and subtle diplomacy to enrich
  themselves while staying publicly unconnected to the trouble they cause; others are unsubtle brigands
  who start tavern brawls or stage public marketplace assassinations.
- Senior clergy renew and expand a growing network of informers, agents, sympathizers, and faithful
  warriors, training and disciplining them and setting spies on their own agents.
- At the highest levels, priests spend their days in ruthless power plays against rival senior clergy for
  control of one of the faith's independent churches.

### Holy Days / Important Ceremonies

| Name | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dedication of the Slain | At any battlefield victory | A believer who slays a foe shouts Garagos's name over the body to dedicate the death to him as an offering. |
| Blood Altar Rite | Whenever prayer is offered | The devout spill drops of blood into the troughs of a stone Blood Altar, promise an act of battle valor, and call on the Master of All Weapons for aid, strength, or guidance. |
| Blood Festival / the Full Blood | Whenever seven or more priests gather | A feast in which at least some food is butchered at or beside the table and eaten while still bloody. Ordination of novices into full priesthood (the Full Blood) must occur at a Blood Festival, and involves dipping the supplicants' hands in the blood of a monster they personally slew and painting their cheeks with Garagos's symbol. |

### Affiliated Orders

- *(Formal)* Brothers of Blood -- the faith's few crusaders, dedicated to crushing the enemies of
  Garagos; membership tends to be short-lived, as constant low-grade war against the church of Tempus
  makes for a short and bloody life.

## Specialty Priests

- **Name/Title:** Bloodreaver
- **Weapon Proficiencies:** Any; favored weapon tentacus (confirmed via real-world 3.5e sourcing,
  matching this book's own repeated emphasis that most clergy of Garagos carry and are skilled with a
  tentacus as both symbol and weapon)
- **Armor & Shield Proficiencies:** Light, Medium, and Heavy (any armor)
- **Major Spheres:** All, Chaos, Charm, Combat, Divination, Law, Protection, Travelers, War
- **Minor Spheres:** Creation, Guardian, Healing, Necromantic, Thought
- **Magical Items:** No limitations (same as clerics)
- **Skills Required:** 1 rank in endurance, 1 rank in tentacus (weapon skill)
- **Skill Bonuses:** +1 bonus on religion (pantheon); +1 bonus on blind-fighting; +1 bonus on
  charioteering
